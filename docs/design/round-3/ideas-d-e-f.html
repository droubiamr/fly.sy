fly.sy · Redesign, round 3 · 1 October 2026

Three looks that only fly.sy could have

Round 2 still looked like an app template, with soft rounded cards and pill buttons on a grey ground. These three start from real objects in a traveller’s hands instead: a passport page, an airport departure board, a transit map. Each has its own buttons, its own background and its own way of showing status.

Same page in all three: Türkiye to Damascus, Syrian passport, in Arabic. The structure from the plan stays. Pick one, or tell me what to take from each.

The page looks like the paper you travel with. The background is the fine security pattern printed on passport pages. You fill in the question like a form and tick your passport type. The answer is printed like a visa sticker with labelled fields, and its status is a rubber stamp with the date it was checked.

Buttons
Ruled, rectangular buttons with a double line, like a printed form box. The main one is solid ink.
Background
Pale passport paper with a guilloché security pattern; a dark passport-cover band on top.
Type
Noto Naskh Arabic (the style used on Arabic forms), Reem Kufi for the logo, Courier Prime for flight numbers and the machine-readable line.
  • Unmistakably about travel papers, which is what the site is about
  • Stamps make status impossible to miss and carry the check date
  • Paper texture needs restraint on long pages
  • Must not look like an official government form

The page is the black board every traveller has stood under. Airport codes and the travel time click over in split-flap tiles. Flights sit in board rows with lit status words. Buttons are physical keys that sink when pressed. One dark look, on purpose.

Buttons
Chunky keys with a visible bottom edge; the chosen key lights up yellow and sits lower.
Background
Matte black board with faint module lines between rows.
Type
Changa for Arabic and Latin, a narrow technical face that reads like signage; tiles for codes and times.
  • Instantly says ‘flights’, and the board layout fits a list of flights naturally
  • High contrast; very legible on a phone in sunlight
  • Dark only; some people prefer light pages
  • Overland crossings fit the board metaphor less well

Each way to Syria is drawn as a metro line, with stops where you change: fly from Istanbul, land at Damascus airport, drive 30 minutes into the city. Each way has its own coloured line badge. A closed crossing is drawn like a closed station, crossed out.

Buttons
Square signage buttons: black name plates for places, hard-edged outlined buttons for choices.
Background
Map paper with a light grid, like a printed network map.
Type
Noto Sans Arabic in heavy weights with Archivo for Latin: plain, strong signage type.
  • Shows the whole journey, each leg and its time, at a glance
  • Line colours and station symbols explain themselves
  • More drawing per route, so more to build and test
  • Line colours must not be confused with status colours

In every direction

What stays the same

  • Real text, not pictures of textStamps, tiles and line badges are styled HTML text, so search engines, AI assistants and screen readers read them like any other words.
  • Status never by colour aloneEvery status has a word and a shape: a stamp, a lit word, or a station symbol.
  • Arabic firstRight to left by default, English mirrored. Fonts self-hosted and split by script.
  • Built from library partsUnder the look: the same Radix and shadcn components from the plan. The look is styling on top of them, not hand-made widgets.
