Three ways to point to Homs Reisen

Homs Reisen (حمص للسياحة والسفر) is a travel office in Berlin, founded in 2018, run by the people who make fly.sy. These are three ways the site can send travellers to it without becoming a sales page: a colophon that says who makes the site, a labelled service plate on the route pages where the office can actually help, and the office as a named source with a declared interest.

Every phone is a mockup in the site's own colours, font, icons and airline logos, in Arabic, opened at the part of the page that changes. What the office is said to do comes from its own website as read on 18 October 2026; nothing on fly.sy has been changed.

Before any option

Three things every option stands on

The site's whole value is that a reader can trust what it says about itself. A referral that contradicts the About page costs more than it earns, so these come first, whichever option you pick.

1. One sentence has to change

“Who is behind fly.sy? An independent project by Syrians in the diaspora. No government body, no airline, no travel agency, and no commission on anything.”

That FAQ, the About page (“sells nothing, takes no commission”), the footer line, README.md, HANDOFF.md, the Organization markup and llms.txt all say it. A reader who finds the office behind the site after reading that line stops believing the data too. So the first change is the disclosure, shipped before or with the first referral, and said as a strength: the people who check the flight boards every week also book these flights.

“Independent” then needs its object: independent of any government body, airline or embassy. Not bare.

2. The office's site decides what fly.sy may say

The site's first writing rule is “say only what the source says”. The office's website (Arabic, with a German legal notice) says, as of today:

  • Berlin, founded 2018; “visas, flight tickets, hotels, tourist trips, Umrah and Hajj”.
  • Flight tickets “to any place in the world” on international airlines; departures listed from Berlin, Hamburg, Frankfurt, Düsseldorf and Munich; Sundair among its partner logos.
  • Staff speak Arabic and German. Contact by WhatsApp, phone, email or the Berlin office; its web forms say sending “starts soon”.
  • Syria is not mentioned anywhere.

So fly.sy can say “tickets, visas and hotels”, but not “books flights to Syria”, until the office publishes a Syria page. That page is also where every link should land, instead of a home page about Umrah.

3. The office is held to the site's own rules

  • It is a commercial source, like ops: whatever it says is capped at “reported”, never “verified”, and the About page says so.
  • Its ratings and customer counts stay on its own site; fly.sy repeats facts, not praise.
  • Anything that invites a booking is labelled as an advertisement and names the company, which German law asks for (see below) and which honesty asks for anyway.
  • One mention per page, never above the answer, and never on Official links, News or Experiences: those pages promise to be about other people.

My recommendation

Ship A with the first referral, make B the referral, and let C grow

A is the floor: who makes the site, said once in the footer and once on the About page. B is where the bookings will come from: one quiet, labelled plate under the facts, only on the route pages with Germany at one end, which hands the traveller's exact plan to the office on WhatsApp. C is the most fly.sy-like idea, but it needs the office to send dated notes every week, so it earns its place over time instead of launching.

  1. Settle the words and the legal notice. The disclosure sentence in both languages (proposed below) and whether fly.sy gets an imprint page. The office is a sole trader (Homs e. K.), so an imprint names its owner; this settles the open question in HANDOFF.md.
  2. The office publishes its Syria page. In Arabic and German: what it books from Germany, what it needs from the traveller, how to reach it. Every link from fly.sy lands there, with a UTM tag per placement.
  3. Ship A. The sentence everywhere it is said today, the “Who makes fly.sy” section, the footer line.
  4. Ship B. data/agency.json, the plate under the papers box on the Germany route pages in both directions, rel="sponsored", the WhatsApp handoff.
  5. After four weeks. Read the office's analytics and decide the Documents page and C.

One decision before anything ships: does the office want to be named on fly.sy at all? Every option names it. If the answer is no, nothing here applies, and the site keeps its current line truthfully only if the office is not in fact behind it.

How it works

  • The footer's independence line (indep in messages/index.ts) and the About page's “What fly.sy is” text change in both languages; the FAQ “Who is behind fly.sy?” gets a new answer. Proposed words are below.
  • The same sentence goes into README.md, HANDOFF.md, the Organization description in schema.ts and the intro of llms.txt, so no copy of the old claim survives anywhere a reader or an assistant looks.
  • The card is the Links page's card shape (header band, body, one row per address), so it reads as a fact about a body, not as a banner. The monogram tile stands in for the office's emblem, which scripts/link-logos.mjs can pull from its site.
  • The link carries ?utm_source=fly.sy&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=colophon, so the office's own analytics show what the About page brings. A plain link, no sponsored: this is a disclosure, not an advertisement.

Good

Costs no trust and adds some: the people who verify the flight boards also book the flights. Nothing on the route pages changes. The lightest legal footprint: it names the company, which is all a disclosure needs.

Watch out

Few people read footers or About pages, so on its own it brings little business. It is a floor, not a referral. It also makes the imprint question real: a site run for a German business usually needs one.

The real Germany → Damascus page as of 8 October, scrolled to the papers box. The plate is the only new thing. Scroll up to see how far below the answer it sits.

Under the papers box, after every fact on the page, a white plate with the green rule set in: the inverse of a way sign. It says the office exists, what it does in its own words, that it is ours, and it offers one action: send this plan on WhatsApp. It appears only on route pages with Germany at one end, in both directions.

How it works

  • A plate, not a sign. On fly.sy green signs are the ways themselves; white plates carry extra information (“Fastest”, “Syrians only”). The office gets a plate, so it never looks like a route. Real road signs do the same: services sit on a different colour from directions.
  • Only where the office can help. data/agency.json lists the office's markets as origin ids (["DE"] to start; Austria or Switzerland only if its site says so). The plate renders when the route's country is in that list, so 22 of the 24 countries' pages stay exactly as they are today.
  • After the facts, never above them. The plate comes after the answer, the signs, the estimates note and the papers. People who have their answer and still want someone to do it are the ones who scroll this far.
  • The handoff carries the plan. The WhatsApp button is a plain link to wa.me/491629171309 with a prefilled message that contains the page's own address, so the office sees exactly what the traveller saw. Nothing is typed twice and nothing is stored on fly.sy. The passport (?p=) can be appended by the existing passport state component.
  • Labelled. “إعلان · من صانعي fly.sy” (“Ad · from the makers of fly.sy”) on the plate, the company named in the text, rel="sponsored noopener noreferrer" on both links, and a UTM tag per placement: utm_campaign=route-plate&utm_content=de-damascus.
  • Says what the office's site says. Today that is tickets, visas, hotels, Arabic and German. The day the office's Syria page is up, the text can become “يحجز الرحلات إلى سوريا من ألمانيا” and the link can land on it.
  • The office's web forms are not live (“starts soon”), so the actions are WhatsApp and the website. English pages show the same plate with “Arabic and German spoken”, so an English reader knows what to expect.
  • Server-rendered, no script, prerendered like the rest of the page; worker.ts and the Workers Free CPU budget are untouched.

Good

The only option that puts the office in front of someone at the moment they are planning that exact trip, and the only one with a real action. Still quiet: one plate, below everything, on 2 of 24 countries, labelled, in the site's own vocabulary.

Watch out

The site now carries an advertisement, so the German rules on marking commercial communication apply in full, and the imprint question stops being optional. It needs the office's Syria page to be worth clicking. And it is the one place a hurried reader might mistake the plate for part of the answer; the label and the colour are what prevent that.

The papers box for a visa-on-arrival passport. The second line is today's anonymous tour-operator line, shown as if the office had sent it in, dated, with the declared-interest plate.

The office contributes what it actually learns from booking: which airline asked for what at the Düsseldorf check-in, how long a pre-approval took for a client, which fares were refundable. Each note is a dated line with the office as its named source, capped at “reported”, with a small plate that says “our office”. The referral is the byline: a reader meets the office at the moment one of its facts helps them.

How it works

  • One new entry in data/sources.json, homs: name, kind “Commercial source, and our own office”, use “What the office sees when booking and at check-in on routes from Germany. A declared interest, so reported, never verified”, and its URL with utm_campaign=source. The About page's source list shows it automatically, with that wording.
  • Lines in needs.json and notes on routes can then cite homs instead of the anonymous ops where the office is the one who knows. Provenance adds the “مكتبنا / our office” plate whenever the source is the office, so the interest is visible at every citation, not only on the About page.
  • Needs get an optional seen date so an office note shows when it was last true, as flights do.
  • The same rules as every source: a note says what happened, with a date, and is re-checked on the weekly pass. Tour-operator claims are capped at reported today; the owner's own office must be too, or the cap means nothing.

Good

The most fly.sy of the three: the office is useful before it is visible, and the declared interest is the site's honesty model applied to itself. Natural links, no advertisement, no label needed. Over months it builds the one thing a travel office cannot buy: a record of being right.

Watch out

It only works if the office really sends dated notes every week; an empty source is worse than none. Slow to bring bookings. And every office note must pass the writing rules: no “always”, no “never”, no consequence the note does not state.

Honesty, law, measurement

What “well studied” means here

Four things to get right whichever option ships. The legal points are the rules as published; they are not legal advice, and the Berlin chamber of commerce (IHK) or a lawyer should confirm them before B goes live.

Say only what the office's site says

  • Today it says: Berlin, 2018, “visas, flight tickets, hotels, tourist trips, Umrah and Hajj”, tickets “to any place in the world”, Arabic and German. It does not say Syria. So fly.sy does not either, until it does.
  • Its Google rating and customer count stay on its site. fly.sy repeats what a body does, not how good it says it is; it does the same for airlines.
  • The landing page matters as much as the plate. A fly.sy reader who lands on an Umrah home page leaves. The office's Syria page, in Arabic and German, with WhatsApp on it, is step 2 of the plan for that reason.
  • Add the rule to CLAUDE.md: the office is a commercial source; cap at reported; one mention per page; never above the answer; never on Official links, News or Experiences.

German rules on marking commercial communication

  • § 6 DDG (in force since 14 May 2024, replacing § 6 TMG): commercial communication must be clearly recognisable as such, and the company on whose behalf it is made clearly identifiable. The plate's label and the company name do both.
  • § 5a (4) UWG: hiding the commercial purpose of a business practice is unfair unless it is obvious from the context. A February 2025 Bayreuth judgment (1 HK O 30/24) applied both rules to undisclosed promotion. “Ad · from the makers of fly.sy” removes the question.
  • § 5 DDG, the imprint: a site that carries commercial communication for a German business is usually a business-like service and needs one, naming the operator. fly.sy has none today. The office's own imprint (Homs e. K., Amtsgericht Charlottenburg, HRA 61161 B) shows the shape; linkat.sy's single-language legal page shows how this codebase does it.
  • The option-A disclosure alone is not advertising, but it names the company, which is what § 6 asks of any mention made on its behalf.

Google and the links

  • Google asks that links which are advertisements carry rel="sponsored". The plate's two links get it; the colophon's link and a source byline are ordinary links.
  • No keyword anchors (“cheap flights to Damascus”): the link text is the office's name or its host, as the Links page does for every body.
  • Outside links open in a new tab with noopener noreferrer, as everywhere on the site.

Measure without surveilling

  • One UTM tag per placement, read in the office's own analytics: utm_source=fly.sy&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=colophon|route-plate|source&utm_content=de-damascus. The office then knows which placement and which route brings people.
  • Nothing new is stored on fly.sy, no script is added, and the WhatsApp message carries only the page address the traveller is already looking at.
  • If click counts on fly.sy's side are wanted later, a /go/homs redirect answered by worker.ts can count them in D1 without a third party, the way page views are counted now.
