Three ways to point travellers to a travel office

Homs Reisen (حمص للسياحة والسفر) is a Berlin travel office you trust and want to send travellers to, as your own recommendation. More offices will follow, each with its own angle: an airline it books, a taxi from the airport, a car to rent. These are three ways the site can do that without becoming a sales page: a page of recommended offices in the Links page's shape, a board under the facts on the route pages where an office can actually help, and an office as a named source. All three read one data file, so the second office is a data edit, and the page is what you will show the next office when you pitch it.

Every phone is a mockup in the site's own colours, font, icons and airline logos, in Arabic, opened at the part of the page that changes. What Homs Reisen is said to do comes from its own website as read on 18 October 2026. Nothing on fly.sy has been changed.

Before any option

Three things every option stands on

The site's whole value is that a reader can trust what it says. A recommendation borrows that trust, so these come first, whichever option you pick.

1. The site's word about itself stays true

“Who is behind fly.sy? An independent project by Syrians in the diaspora. No government body, no airline, no travel agency, and no commission on anything.”

Nothing here changes that: no office is behind the site, and none pays. One sentence is added to it: that the site recommends travel offices it knows and trusts. The recommendation is the site's own, like the opinions already in its notes (“a dependable fallback when the direct flights don't fit”), and it is not marked as anything else. HANDOFF.md already says what happens to the “no commission” line if money ever changes hands; that stays your call and is not built here.

2. An office's own site decides what fly.sy may say

The site's first writing rule is “say only what the source says”. For Homs Reisen the source is homs-reisen.de (Arabic, with a German legal notice), which says, as of today:

  • Berlin, founded 2018; “visas, flight tickets, hotels, tourist trips, Umrah and Hajj”.
  • Flight tickets “to any place in the world”; departures listed from Berlin, Hamburg, Frankfurt, Düsseldorf and Munich; Sundair among its partner logos.
  • Staff speak Arabic and German. Contact by WhatsApp, phone, email or the Berlin office; its web forms say sending “starts soon”.
  • Syria is not mentioned anywhere.

So fly.sy can say “tickets, visas and hotels” and “Sundair among its partners”, but not “books flights to Syria”, until the office's site does. The same rule gives every later office its chips: an airport taxi or a rental car appears on its card only in the words of its own site.

3. What a recommendation means, written down

Short, and on the page, so a traveller knows what it is and an office knows what it gets:

  • Offices you know, have dealt with, or have checked yourself. The card says what each offers in the office's own words, and when its details were last checked.
  • What an office says is a commercial source: capped at “reported”, never “verified”. Its ratings and customer counts stay on its own site.
  • One board per page, after the facts, never above the answer, and never on Official links, News or Experiences: those pages promise to be about other people.
  • The contract is between the traveller and the office. The site's disclaimer already covers the rest.

My recommendation

Build A and B together on one data file; let C grow

A is the page: every office, what it offers in its own words, its contact rows, its date. B is where the bookings come from: a board under the papers box on the route pages of the office's markets, one row per office, with a WhatsApp button that hands over the traveller's exact plan. B needs A to point at (“why we recommend them”), and A without B is a page nobody finds. C is the most fly.sy-like idea, but it needs an office to send dated notes, so it earns its place over time instead of launching.

  1. Write the words. The added FAQ sentence, the page's lede and its “why these offices” paragraph, the board's title. Proposed below, in both languages.
  2. Ask Homs Reisen. Whether it wants to be listed, who answers its WhatsApp, and whether it will put up a Syria page in Arabic and German for the links to land on.
  3. Ship A and B together. data/agencies.json, the offices page, the board on the Germany route pages in both directions, the footer link, the FAQ sentence.
  4. After four weeks, pull the numbers. The admin dashboard counts views per page, so the pitch to the next office can say how many people looked at its market's pages last month, and what an office on fly.sy gets (below).
  5. C when an office sends notes. One source entry, the tag on its lines, nothing else.

One decision before anything ships: the order of rows when two offices share a market. File order (the order you added them) or the check date are both explainable; decide now, before an office asks.

The pitch, in one breath

What an office on fly.sy gets, and what it never gets

This is what you tell the next office. The second column is what keeps the first worth having.

An office on fly.sy gets

  • A row on every route page of its markets, both directions, both languages, under the facts: the moment someone is planning that exact trip.
  • A card on the offices page with what it offers and the date it was last checked, so travellers can see the listing is alive.
  • Its own angle, in its own words: the airlines it names, an airport taxi, a rental car, visas, hotels, Umrah. An airline it names as a partner shows that carrier's tile on routes that carrier flies.
  • A WhatsApp button that opens with the traveller's plan already written: the route page's address, and the passport.
  • Tagged traffic it can see in its own analytics, one tag per placement, and the month's view count for its market from the dashboard.

It never gets

  • A say in the facts, or a line changed, added or removed for it.
  • A “verified” grade: what it says stays “reported”, like every operator.
  • A place above the answer, a green sign, or a place on the Official links, News or Experiences pages.
  • Its ratings or customer counts repeated on fly.sy, or a chip its own site does not back.
  • A row that outlives the recommendation.

A new page in the Links page's shape: a short lede, one card per office, a line for offices that want in, a footer link. Scroll the phone.

A page of its own, /agencies, built like the Links page: a short lede saying what the page is, then one card per office with its city, its languages, what it offers in its own words, its contact rows, and the date its details were last checked. The footer links to it; the route pages link to it from the papers box, or through B. The last line invites offices to write in, which is where the later pitch starts.

How it works

  • One data file. data/agencies.json, one record per office: names, city, founding year, languages, what it offers (text in its own words), services and airlines as codes for the chips, markets as origin ids, its links, the page that vouches for it (via), and seen. A second office is one more record. The shape is below.
  • Chips in the office's words. “Partner: Sundair” because its site lists Sundair among its partners; tickets, visas, hotels, Umrah because its site sells them. A later office with an airport taxi gets a taxi chip the same way. No chip without a sentence on the office's own site.
  • The date is the promise. As on the Links page, seen is the day the card's addresses were opened and its text compared with the office's site. A card nobody has re-checked for months says so by itself.
  • Not on the Links page: that page promises official bodies, and a commercial card there would cost it that promise. A new page keeps the two apart, and gives offices a page to be found on.
  • Links carry utm_source=fly.sy&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=agencies. The office's emblem comes through scripts/link-logos.mjs like the emblems on the Links page.

Good

The structure the site already uses for bodies it vouches for, so it reads as a fact page and not as a banner. Scales to twenty offices without a redesign. It is the page you will show the next office.

Watch out

A page nobody is sent to is a page nobody reads; it needs B, or at least a link from the papers box. The date is a promise that takes a few minutes every quarter per office.

The real Germany → Damascus page as of 8 October, scrolled to the papers box. The board is the only new thing. Scroll up to see how far below the answer it sits.

Under the papers box, after every fact on the page, a white board like the flights board: a question as its title, one row per office that serves this route's country, and a WhatsApp button on each row. The row carries the office's city, its languages and its chips; an airline it names as a partner shows that carrier's tile when the carrier flies this route. Nothing is green, nothing sits above the answer, and 22 of the 24 countries' pages do not change.

How it works

  • A board, not a sign. Green signs are the ways themselves; boards are information that goes with them (the flights board, the papers box). The offices get a board, so a hurried reader never takes them for a route.
  • Rows by market. The board lists the offices whose markets include the route's country, in both directions: Germany → Damascus and Damascus → Germany. One office today, several later; the board grows a row, not a redesign.
  • The handoff carries the plan. The WhatsApp button is a plain link to the office's wa.me number with a prefilled message that names fly.sy and contains the page's own address, so the office knows where the traveller came from and what they saw, and nothing is typed twice or stored on fly.sy. The passport (?p=) can be appended by the existing passport state component.
  • The chips tie the office to the route. “Partner: Sundair” appears on this page because Sundair is on this page's flights board and on the office's partner list. On a page Sundair does not fly, the chip stays off. A taxi or car-rental chip from a later office shows wherever that office's markets apply.
  • “Why we recommend them” links to the offices page (A). Links carry utm_campaign=route-board&utm_content=de-damascus.
  • Server-rendered, no script, prerendered like the rest of the page; worker.ts and the Workers Free CPU budget are untouched.

Good

The only option that puts an office in front of someone at the moment they are planning that exact trip, with a real action. It is also the thing you will pitch: a row on the pages of a market. Still quiet: one board, below everything, only on the markets an office serves, in the site's own vocabulary.

Watch out

The board is worth clicking only if the office's own site has something to land on; today that is an Umrah home page. With several offices in one market, their order becomes a thing offices will ask about. And a board written like an advert reads like one, so the row stays to facts: what the office sells, where, in which languages.

The papers box for a visa-on-arrival passport. The second line is today's anonymous tour-operator line, shown as if the office had sent it in, dated, with the “travel office” tag.

An office contributes what it actually learns from booking: which airline asked for what at the Düsseldorf check-in, how long a pre-approval took for a client, which fares were refundable. Each note is a dated line with the office as its named source, capped at “reported”, with a small tag that says it is a travel office. The referral is the byline: a reader meets the office at the moment one of its facts helps them.

How it works

  • One entry per contributing office in data/sources.json: name, kind “Travel office; a commercial source”, use “What the office sees when booking and at check-in on routes from Germany. A commercial source, so reported, never verified”, and its URL with utm_campaign=source. The About page's source list shows it automatically, with that wording.
  • Lines in needs.json and notes on routes can then cite the office instead of the anonymous ops where the office is the one who knows. Provenance adds the “مكتب سفر / travel office” tag whenever the source is an office, so the interest is visible at every citation.
  • Needs get an optional seen date so an office note shows when it was last true, as flights do.
  • The same rules as every source: a note says what happened, with a date, and is re-read on the weekly pass. Tour-operator claims are capped at reported today; a recommended office's must be too, or the cap means nothing.

Good

The most fly.sy of the three: an office is useful before it is visible, and the tag is the site's honesty model applied to a commercial source. Natural links, no banner. Over months it builds the one thing an office cannot buy: a record of being right.

Watch out

It only works if an office really sends dated notes; an empty source is worse than none. Slow to bring bookings. Every office note must pass the writing rules: no “always”, no “never”, no consequence the note does not state.

Honesty, measurement, the one thing to know

What “well studied” means here

Say only what the office's site says

  • Homs Reisen's site says: Berlin, 2018, “visas, flight tickets, hotels, tourist trips, Umrah and Hajj”, tickets “to any place in the world”, Arabic and German, Sundair among its partners. It does not say Syria. So fly.sy does not either, until it does.
  • Every chip on every later card has the same test: a sentence on the office's own site. “Taxi from the airport” needs the office to say so where a traveller can read it.
  • Ratings and customer counts stay on the office's site. fly.sy repeats what a body does, not how good it says it is; it does the same for airlines.
  • The landing page matters as much as the row. A reader who lands on an Umrah home page leaves. Asking Homs Reisen for a Syria page, in Arabic and German, is step 2 of the plan for that reason.
  • Add the rule to CLAUDE.md: offices are commercial sources; cap at reported; chips only in the office's words; one board per page, after the facts; never on Official links, News or Experiences.

Measure without surveilling, and the numbers for the pitch

  • One UTM tag per placement, read in the office's own analytics: utm_source=fly.sy&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=agencies|route-board|source&utm_content=de-damascus. The WhatsApp message names fly.sy, so a chat lead is attributed too.
  • Nothing new is stored on fly.sy, no script is added, and the WhatsApp message carries only the page address the traveller is already looking at.
  • The pitch's numbers already exist: /admin counts views per path, so “the Germany pages had N views in the last 30 days” is one query over page_views for paths starting /from/germany/ and /en/from/germany/, plus the leaving pages. A per-market figure in the dashboard would make it a glance.
  • If click counts on fly.sy's side are wanted later, a /go/<office> redirect answered by worker.ts can count them in D1 without a third party, the way page views are counted now.

Google and the links

  • A recommendation you make for free is an ordinary link; nothing to mark.
  • No keyword anchors (“cheap flights to Damascus”): the link text is the office's name or its host, as the Links page does for every body.
  • Outside links open in a new tab with noopener noreferrer, as everywhere on the site.

For the record, since you said it is yours to handle

  • As your own unpaid recommendation, German law does not treat the row as advertising: § 5a (4) UWG says an act for another company has no commercial purpose when no payment or similar consideration is received or promised for it (text), and the definition of commercial communication in § 2 DDG leaves out statements made independently and without financial consideration (text). Nothing here is built for the other case, as you asked.
  • Those pages were unreachable when this was written (HTTP 503), so this is a summary from memory, not a quote, and not legal advice.

The added FAQ sentence; a new agencies group (title, lede, note, apply line, board title and subtitle, the service names). Both languages, typed side by side as today.

One source entry per contributing office; the “travel office” tag whenever a line cites one; an optional seen on needs. Existing tests already check that every source id resolves.

The pitch's numberssrc/app/admin/page.tsx, admin-data.tsB

Nothing in the first release; later, a views-per-market figure in the dashboard, which is one grouped query over page_views.
