Five ways to search from Syria

Today every search on fly.sy ends in Syria: 24 countries to Syria's 5 airports, 240 pages. These five options add the other direction, so someone in Damascus can look up Saudi Arabia the way someone in Riyadh looks up Damascus.

Every phone is a mockup in the site's own colours, font, icons, airline logos and map code, in Arabic. Option A works: tap the round arrows on the green sign. The leaving pages show today's arrivals turned around to fill the layout; the real ones would show only departures checked on the airports' own boards. Nothing on the site has been changed.

My recommendation

Build A, your swap button, and add C beside it

A answers the question people will ask, “Damascus to Saudi Arabia”, on the page they already know, with the button every flight app has. C answers the question just before it, “where can I fly from Damascus?”, on the airport pages, for little work. Both run on the same new information, and neither needs a new tab.

  1. Collect the departures first. In the weekly check, read each airport's departures board too and record the way out for every route, with its own source and date. Nothing leaving-related goes on the site before that.
  2. Ship A. The swap button, the leaving route pages, and a Before-you-leave box in place of the passport plates.
  3. Ship C. A Departures tab on the Damascus, Aleppo and Deir ez-Zor pages. It's the smaller job, so it can also go first if you want something live sooner.

One decision before A goes live: does the site stay “How to get into Syria”, or become “to and from Syria”? It's in the title, the share image and the About page.

Arrow style

The button you drew: two arrows on the line between From and To. Tap it and the two places trade rows, and everything under the sign turns around with them. Try it on the phone.

How it works

  • The button is a link to the reverse page, /from/damascus/to/saudi-arabia. It works before the page's script has loaded, and each direction is a page of its own that Google can show.
  • After a swap, From lists Syria's airports (Damascus, Aleppo, Deir ez-Zor; Latakia and Qamishli greyed out while they're closed) and To lists the 24 countries by region, as it does today.
  • Syria's airports also go at the top of the From list. Someone in Damascus who never notices the button still finds their city there.
  • Leaving pages swap the three passport plates for a Before you leave box: the visa for where you're going, and Syria's exit rules. Scroll the phone to see it.
  • Flags on the sign get a thin white edge. Today Saudi Arabia's green flag disappears into the green sign.

Good

Everyone knows this button from flight apps. No new tab and no new kind of page: leaving pages reuse today's route page, map and signs. It answers “Damascus to Saudi Arabia” directly, with door-to-door time.

Watch out

Easy to miss on the home page, which opens on Türkiye → Damascus; putting Syria's airports at the top of the From list covers that. On the leaving side the road comes first and the flight second, so the signs' wording turns around too.

The data underneath

None of them work until the site knows the departures

The site lists 91 routes into Syria, each checked against an airport's arrivals board, an airline or flight tracking. Turning them around automatically would break its first rule, say only what the source says, and the way out isn't always the way in turned around:

Some flights don't come back the same way

Syrian Air's RB342 from Kuwait lands in Deir ez-Zor and continues to Damascus as RB134, and from October swaps between the two airports. Which flight returns to Kuwait, and from where, isn't on the site.

Land crossings have rules per direction

The ports authority's 12 May post on Tal Abyad is about crossing towards Türkiye; its 1 January post lets Syrians with a Turkish residence or work permit enter and leave through six crossings. Lebanon publishes its own rules for Syrians entering Lebanon. Different facts, with their own sources.

The papers are a different list

Leaving means the visa for where you're going (the Links page already lists IATA's Travel Centre) and Syria's own exit rules. Military-service rules are on the site's list of things not published without a dated official source, so that line starts as a declared gap.

Three ways to store it

Don'tTurn the arrivals around automatically

Quick, but it publishes routes nobody checked.

RecommendedA “back” stamp on each arrival row

The weekly check already opens each airport's board; departures are the next tab. One row per route keeps the airline, city and time together, and the way out gets its own status, source and date. A row without the stamp doesn't appear on leaving pages.

If neededA separate departures file

Handles one-way and departures-only routes, but it's twice as many rows to keep in step.

An example row with the stamp (the values in back are made up):

{"airline":"XY", "city":{"en":"Riyadh", …}, "entry":"DAM", "from":"SA",
 "status":"open", "source":"damairport", "seen":"2026-09-30",
 "back":{"status":"open", "source":"damairport", "seen":"2026-10-14",
         "note":{"en":"Twice daily on the departures board.", …}}}

Around the site

What else adding the other direction touches

Whichever option you pick, these parts of the site change with it.

The signplanner.tsx

The swap link; From and To lists that change with the direction; the “with a connection” hints and greyed-out places worked out for each direction.

Route pages and URLs/from/[origin]/to/[city]

The same route file can serve both directions. Country slugs (turkiye, saudi-arabia) and Syrian city ids (damascus, aleppo) never collide, so /from/damascus/to/saudi-arabia needs no new URL scheme. worker.ts serves prerendered pages from static files, so 240 more cost no extra CPU on Workers Free.

The plannersrc/lib/plan.ts

Leaving journeys put the road first (city to airport or crossing), then the flight. Ranking stays the same. It needs its own tests.

The mapworld-map.tsx

Same framing. The line draws from Syria outwards and the label's arrow points the other way.

Way signsway-signs.tsx, messages

The words turn around: “Flights from Damascus airport”, “By road, then from Aleppo airport”, “30 min by road to the airport”.

Passports and papersneeds.json, passport-ui.tsx

Syrian, visa on arrival and pre-approval are about entering Syria. Leaving pages need a Before-you-leave box: the visa for where you're going (the Links page already lists visa checkers) and Syria's exit rules.

Phone tab barmain-nav.tsx

Already 7 tabs of about 51px each, with no room for an 8th. Leaving belongs under Trip (رحلتي), which is where every option puts it.

Site searchsrc/lib/search.ts

Today's country group is “From a country to Damascus”. It needs “From Syria” entries too.

Sitemap, dates, AI filespageUpdated, llms.ts, /data/*.json

pageUpdated only understands /from/<country>/to/<city>; leaving pages would fall back to the review date until it learns them. llms.txt, llms-full.txt and the open data need the departures.

What the site says it ismessages, share image, manifest

“كيف تصل إلى سوريا / How to get into Syria” is the site title, the meta description, the text baked into the share image, the manifest, and the About page (“answers one question: how do I get into Syria today?”). Decide whether it stays “into Syria” with leaving as a feature, or becomes “to and from Syria” (من وإلى سوريا).

Experiences formreport-form.tsx, D1

It asks “Where did you enter?”. Reports about leaving need a direction field: a small database migration.

One more board per airport to read: departures.

Site check · 7 October 2026

How the site is doing today

I ran the site's own checks, opened every page at phone width, and read the code and the data against the site's writing rules (CLAUDE.md). The site builds, loads and passes its tests. What needs fixing is mostly wording and data, plus a few places where parts of a page disagree with each other. Every item below was checked against the files, the built pages or the cited source.

✓

Lint, types, tests and build pass. 53 of 53 tests; the production build prerenders 363 pages.

✓

Crawler smoke test: 0 failures. All 338 sitemap addresses: status, canonical, hreflang, one heading, structured data, redirects and 404s.

✓

Phone check: nothing wider than the screen. Every page at 390px wide in light mode, and 49 of them in dark, with no script errors.

✓

The live site answers. The home page, a route page and the sitemap all return 200. The home page is 38 KB over the wire.

Worth fixing, most important first

  1. high

    The visa-on-arrival line shows on crossings where it doesn't apply

    data/needs.json (land, visa on arrival) says “The visa is issued at the crossing: a short form and a stamp, in and out.” Every crossing page shows it, including the six Türkiye crossings, whose own notes say foreign visitors are generally not admitted, and Semalka, Al-Yarubiyah and Jisr Qamar, where entry for foreigners isn't confirmed. Show it only where the source says it applies.

  2. high

    Tal Abyad: the source says one direction, the site says both

    The note in entries.json says passports “have been accepted both ways since 12 May 2026”. The ports authority post it rests on (t.me/SyrGABC/2966) announces crossing “باتجاه تركيا”, towards Türkiye only, and the Şanlıurfa → Tal Abyad route into Syria depends on it. Look for a second post about entry before keeping that route.

  3. high

    Closed airlines are described as flying

    Google's snippet for the Emirates page reads “Emirates routes into Syria today: Dubai → Damascus airport”, but Emirates suspended Damascus in November 2025. The same goes for Saudia, SalamAir and Sundair's Berlin–Aleppo. The list “Other airlines flying to Syria” includes all of them, and llms.txt lists their routes without a status.

  4. medium

    The “Fastest” sign can disagree with the answer

    On Iraq → Deir ez-Zor the answer says “fly to Aleppo airport, about 6.5 hours”. The sign marked Fastest, opened by default, is Abu Kamal (conditional, 4 h), and the papers box shows the overland list. The sentence prefers routes that simply run; the sign and the papers box don't.

  5. medium

    With a foreign passport, no sign says “Fastest”

    On Türkiye → Latakia with visa on arrival, the open sign with the times and logos is Kasab, which is for Syrians only, moved to the bottom. The Aleppo flights the answer names stay closed, and the map still draws the Syrians-only crossings. The main sign is chosen once, for a Syrian passport.

  6. medium

    The destination list says “direct” when it isn't

    Egypt, France, the UK, Sweden, Norway, Switzerland and Belgium show Damascus without “برحلة موصولة” (with a connection), because their only rows are connections and those count as direct. Qatar shows Aleppo as direct on an unconfirmed row.

  7. medium

    The Türkiye papers line says there's no official source, but there is one

    needs.json (land, Syrian) says “We have no current official source”. The News page carries the ports authority's 1 January 2026 post: Syrians in Türkiye with a residence or work permit can enter and leave through six crossings.
